Sleep, Breathing and Evening Habits That Help You Rest

How bedroom air, sleep position and a wind-down routine affect how you breathe at night, and when to talk to a doctor about snoring.

By the Pulmo Balance editorial team · Published 25 Nov 2026

How you breathe at night affects how rested you feel in the morning. A few changes to your bedroom and evening routine can make sleep more comfortable.

Set Up the Bedroom

  • Cool and fresh: a slightly cool room is more comfortable for most people. Air the room out before bed.
  • Clean bedding: wash sheets and pillowcases weekly to reduce dust mites.
  • Humidity: in dry months, a clean humidifier can keep your nose and throat from drying out.

Sleep Position

Some people breathe more comfortably on their side than on their back, and a slightly raised head can help. Experiment to find what works for you.

A Wind-Down Routine

Dim the lights, put screens away 30–60 minutes before bed, and try an extended-exhale breathing exercise from our breathing exercises guide. Avoid heavy meals and alcohol close to bedtime.

Snoring and When to See a Doctor

Occasional snoring is common. But loud, regular snoring, pauses in breathing, gasping or waking unrefreshed can be signs of sleep apnea, which needs medical assessment. Don't rely on supplements or gadgets for these symptoms.
