Simple Ways to Improve the Air You Breathe at Home

Most of us spend most of our time indoors. These practical steps target the biggest sources of indoor air problems.

By the Pulmo Balance editorial team · Updated November 2026 · Educational content, not medical advice

We spend most of our time indoors, so the air inside your home matters a great deal. The good news is that the most effective improvements are free or cheap.

1. Start with the source

The US Environmental Protection Agency names pollutant sources as the main cause of indoor air problems, and poor ventilation makes them worse. Common sources include tobacco smoke, gas or wood-burning appliances, strong cleaning products and building materials. See the EPA's Introduction to Indoor Air Quality (US Environmental Protection Agency).

  • Keep your home and car smoke-free.
  • Choose low-fume cleaning products and use them with a window open.
  • Have fuel-burning appliances serviced regularly.

2. Ventilate on purpose

Run an extractor fan while cooking and showering, and open windows when the outdoor air is good. Bringing fresh air in dilutes pollutants that build up indoors.

3. Control dust and damp

Vacuum with a well-sealed machine, wash bedding regularly and fix leaks quickly to prevent mould.

4. Watch the outdoor air

On high-pollution or high-pollen days, keep windows shut during peak hours and save heavy outdoor exercise for cleaner days.

Where supplements fit: no capsule can filter the air you breathe. A product like Pulmo Balance can be one small part of a wider plan that starts with cleaner air. Find out more on our Everyday Air Exposure page.
