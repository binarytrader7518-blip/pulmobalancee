Simple Ways to Improve Indoor Air Quality at Home

Practical, low-cost steps for cleaner indoor air: ventilation, humidity, cleaning habits, cooking fumes and when an air purifier makes sense.

By the Pulmo Balance editorial team · Published 25 Nov 2026

Most of us spend the majority of our time indoors, so the air at home matters. The good news: many of the most effective improvements are free or inexpensive.

Ventilate Every Day

Opening windows for even 10–15 minutes swaps stale indoor air for fresh air. Use extractor fans in the kitchen and bathroom, especially while cooking or showering. On high-pollen or high-pollution days, ventilate at quieter times, such as after rain.

Deal With Cooking Fumes

Gas stoves and high-heat cooking release particles and gases. Use the range hood on its highest practical setting and keep it running for a few minutes after you finish.

Keep Humidity in a Comfortable Range

Air that's too dry can make your nose and throat feel scratchy, while damp air encourages mold and dust mites. Aim for comfortable, moderate humidity: run a clean humidifier in dry winter months and fix leaks and condensation promptly.

Clean Smarter

  • Vacuum with a HEPA-filter vacuum and damp-dust surfaces so particles don't go airborne.
  • Choose fragrance-free cleaning products and avoid mixing chemicals.
  • Wash bedding weekly in hot water.

Keep Smoke Out

Tobacco smoke is the biggest indoor air pollutant you can control. Keep your home smoke-free, and be thoughtful about candles, incense and wood-burning stoves.

When an Air Purifier Helps

A purifier with a true HEPA filter, sized for the room, can reduce airborne particles, which is useful during wildfire smoke or pollen season. It works alongside ventilation, not instead of it.
