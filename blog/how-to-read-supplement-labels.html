How to Read a Supplement Label Before You Buy

Serving sizes, proprietary blends and marketing claims: a five-minute checklist for any supplement.

By the Pulmo Balance editorial team · Updated November 2026 · Educational content, not medical advice

Supplement labels pack in a lot of information, along with plenty of marketing. Here's how to read one in five minutes, using guidance from the NIH Office of Dietary Supplements: Dietary Supplements: What You Need to Know (Office of Dietary Supplements).

1. Find the Supplement Facts panel

Every dietary supplement label should have a Supplement Facts panel listing the serving size, servings per container and amount of each ingredient. Where a "proprietary blend" is used, only the blend's total weight may be shown.

2. Check the serving size

Work out how many capsules make one serving and how long a bottle will last. For example, two capsules a day from a 60-capsule bottle is a 30-day supply.

3. Read claims critically

As the FDA explains in FDA 101: Dietary Supplements (US Food and Drug Administration), supplements are not approved by the FDA for safety or effectiveness before they're sold. Phrases like "supports" or "promotes" describe intended use. They aren't proof of results.

4. Look for safety information

Check who shouldn't take the product, such as during pregnancy, nursing or with certain medications, and talk with your health care providers. NCCIH's 5 Tips: What Consumers Need To Know About Dietary Supplements (National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health) is a good checklist.
