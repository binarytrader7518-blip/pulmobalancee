Product details

What's Inside Every Capsule

These are the seven ingredients the seller names publicly. Amounts per serving have not been published.

Mullein extract

Verbascum thapsus

A tall flowering herb whose leaves and flowers have a long history in traditional herbal practice for the throat and chest.

What research says: Reviews of mullein describe flavonoids, saponins and other plant compounds studied mostly in laboratory settings. Controlled human trials are scarce, so its role rests mainly on traditional use.

Bromelain

from Ananas comosus (pineapple)

A group of protein-digesting enzymes taken from the stem and fruit of the pineapple plant.

What research says: NIH's NCCIH notes that evidence for oral bromelain is limited. It is generally well tolerated; stomach upset is the most common side effect, and it may interact with some medicines.

Maritime pine bark extract

Pinus pinaster

A polyphenol-rich extract made from the bark of maritime pine trees that grow around the Mediterranean.

What research says: MedlinePlus rates one specific pine bark extract (Pycnogenol) as possibly effective for asthma symptoms when used alongside asthma medicine, never in place of it. The seller does not state which pine bark extract it uses.

Stinging nettle extract

Urtica dioica

A leafy perennial plant used in traditional herbal practice across Europe, Asia and North America.

What research says: The National Cancer Institute's drug dictionary describes nettle extract as having potential antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity, based mainly on preclinical work.

Tiger milk mushroom

Lignosus rhinocerus

A medicinal mushroom from Southeast Asia, traditionally used by indigenous communities for respiratory complaints.

What research says: A 3-month open-label study of 50 adults reported changes in respiratory symptoms and antioxidant markers. It had no placebo group, so the findings are preliminary.

Quercetin dihydrate

plant flavonoid

A flavonoid pigment found naturally in onions, apples, berries and tea.

What research says: Reviews describe quercetin's antioxidant activity in laboratory research. Human results are mixed and no recommended daily intake has been set.

BioPerine

piperine from black pepper

A branded black-pepper extract standardised for piperine, added to formulas to help absorption of other ingredients.

What research says: Piperine is widely studied as an absorption enhancer. Because it can also change how some medicines are absorbed, anyone taking prescription drugs should check with a pharmacist.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Pulmo Balance?

Pulmo Balance is a dietary supplement in capsule form made for adults who want to support everyday respiratory wellness. It combines seven named botanical and nutrient ingredients, including mullein, bromelain and maritime pine bark. It is a supplement, not a medicine. See the full product overview.

What ingredients are in Pulmo Balance?

The seller names seven ingredients: mullein extract, bromelain, maritime pine bark extract, stinging nettle extract, tiger milk mushroom, quercetin dihydrate and BioPerine (black pepper extract). Some brand materials describe a 13-ingredient blend, and individual amounts per serving are not published. Read about each ingredient.

How does Pulmo Balance work?

The formula brings together plant ingredients that have been studied for antioxidant activity and that have a long history of traditional use for the throat and chest. BioPerine is included to help with absorption. Research on these ingredients was done on each one separately, not on the finished formula. Learn how it fits your routine.

What are the intended benefits?

It is designed to support breathing comfort, normal airway function, antioxidant defences and resilience against everyday environmental exposure. Individual results vary, and it is not intended to diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease. See the benefits page.

How do I take Pulmo Balance?

The seller's directions are two capsules daily with a meal and a glass of water. Do not exceed the recommended amount. View the directions.

How long does one bottle last?

One bottle is a 30-day supply at the recommended two capsules a day. The seller lists the 2-bottle package as a 60-day supply, 3 bottles as 90 days and 6 bottles as 180 days.

How long does it take to notice a difference?

The seller recommends taking it consistently for four months or longer. Botanical supplements tend to work gradually, and experiences differ from person to person, so no specific timeline can be promised.

Are there side effects, and who should avoid it?

The seller says the product is not intended for anyone under 18 or for women who are pregnant or nursing. Bromelain can cause stomach upset, and bromelain and piperine can interact with some medicines. Speak with your doctor or pharmacist first if you take medication, have a medical condition or have surgery planned.
