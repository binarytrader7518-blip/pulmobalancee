Antioxidant Defense & Circulation

What maritime pine bark and quercetin bring as plant antioxidants, and what the research does and doesn't show.

Why This Area Matters

Your lungs process thousands of liters of air each day, and with it come pollutants and other sources of oxidative stress. The body has its own antioxidant defenses, and diet contributes too. Plant antioxidants are a popular supplement category, but it helps to understand what the research does and doesn't show.

Related ingredients

What Pulmo Balance Includes for Antioxidant Defense

Maritime Pine Bark

Pinus pinaster

Maritime pine bark extract is rich in proanthocyanidins. The seller includes it as an antioxidant that supports healthy circulation in the lungs. MedlinePlus summarizes evidence ratings for a specific pine bark extract across several uses, with many results still uncertain.

Quercetin Dihydrate

Plant flavonoid

Quercetin appears in many colorful foods. Reviews describe its antioxidant activity, but much of the evidence comes from laboratory work, and quercetin on its own is poorly absorbed.

Research on individual ingredients does not prove the finished product works. See the references.

Everyday habits

Habits That Support This Area

  • Eat a variety of colorful fruit and vegetables, such as onions, apples, berries and leafy greens.
  • Limit exposure to traffic fumes and smoke where you can.
  • Don't smoke or vape; it's the single biggest step for lung health.
  • Exercise outdoors away from busy roads when possible.
