Airway & Breathing Comfort

Comfortable breathing depends on clear, well-hydrated airways. Two ingredients in the formula, mullein and bromelain, are most often associated with the throat and chest.

Mullein: a traditional chest herb

Mullein's soft leaves and yellow flowers have been used in herbal practice for centuries. Scientific reviews describe its flavonoids, saponins and other compounds, but most of that research is experimental rather than clinical. Mullein's place in the formula rests mainly on its long tradition.

Bromelain: the pineapple enzyme

Bromelain is a mix of protein-digesting enzymes from pineapple. NIH's NCCIH says the evidence for oral bromelain is limited and that it's generally well tolerated. Stomach upset is the most common side effect.

Habits that matter

Drink plenty of water, avoid smoke, and use extractor fans while cooking. If your breathing changes, see a doctor promptly. A supplement can't treat a respiratory condition.

Key ingredients

Mullein extract

Reviews of mullein describe flavonoids, saponins and other plant compounds studied mostly in laboratory settings. Controlled human trials are scarce, so its role rests mainly on traditional use.

Bromelain

NIH's NCCIH notes that evidence for oral bromelain is limited. It is generally well tolerated; stomach upset is the most common side effect, and it may interact with some medicines.
